One Direction to Rule Them All: Audio and Text Share the Same Safety Geometry

We show that the 1-dimensional refusal subspace identified in text-input LLMs transfers to the audio modality in Qwen2-Audio-7B — enabling cross-modal mechanistic interventions that bypass refusal on audio inputs.

Black-box adversarial probe — layer 31

Unexpected finding: AJailBench black-box adversarial audio — real waveforms with perturbations embedded in the signal — clusters near harmless activations, not near harmful. The probe achieves 98.9% accuracy, but adversarial audio occupies a distinct region from direct harmful requests, suggesting the perturbation bypasses the refusal direction entirely.
Causal Intervention

Cross-Modal Ablation

Ablating the text-derived refusal direction from audio activations at all layers during generation. If the same 1D subspace mediates refusal across modalities, this should bypass refusal on audio inputs without any audio-specific fine-tuning.

How It Works

TTS / Real audio
Kokoro TTS, FLEURS real speech, or AJailBench adversarial waveforms → 16kHz
🔊
Whisper
Whisper-Large-V2 encoder → 1280-dim states
🔗
Projector
Qwen2-Audio trained projector → 4096-dim LLM space
🧠
HookedTransformer
Qwen1.5-7B shell + Qwen2-Audio LM weights injected layer by layer
📐
Difference of Means
Refusal direction = mean(harmful) − mean(harmless) at last token position
✂️
Ablation
Project out refusal direction from all residual stream positions at all layers

Audio tokens are injected via hook_embed — the intercept point between the embedding lookup table and layer 0 — so all downstream TransformerLens hooks operate identically to the text pathway.
